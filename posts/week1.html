The Marvel Archipelago

We expected a single superhero hairball. Instead, the frozen Wikipedia snapshot has a giant continent, one small island, and 17 characters completely cut off from the rest.

September 2026 · YOUR GROUP NAME

What we asked

How connected is this universe really? The network feels dense because famous characters link everywhere, but “303 nodes and 1,784 arrows” does not tell us whether everybody belongs to the same connected world. We therefore ignored direction temporarily, found the connected components, and then brought direction back to compare two different kinds of hubs.

What we did

We loaded all 303 characters before adding any edges so isolated nodes could not disappear. The directed snapshot contains 1,784 links. Collapsing reciprocal arrows into undirected pairs leaves 1,434 connected pairs; we used that undirected view only for the component calculation.

The first surprise: 277 characters—about 91% of the network—sit in one giant component. Outside it are one 9-character component and 17 isolates. That means 26 characters sit outside the giant component even though the network looks overwhelmingly connected at first glance.

What surprised us

The network has two very different answers to “who is a hub?” Spider-Man receives 106 incoming links, while Betsy Braddock has the largest out-degree at 28. That difference makes sense once we remember who creates the links: an incoming link is many other pages deciding a character matters; an outgoing link is one page's editors deciding what that page should point to.

The component result was stranger. A force-directed drawing can make the giant component dominate our attention, but a component calculation exposes the exceptions immediately. The 17 isolates are especially interesting because a careless edge-list-only import would delete them from the dataset entirely.

One thing we would try next

Identify the nine characters in the small island and inspect their pages. Is the island a meaningful Marvel sub-universe, a naming artifact, or just a frozen-snapshot accident? That is a better next question than making the giant hairball prettier.
